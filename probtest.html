Running mean

Running mean X̄n (this run) Earlier runs μ ± 2σ/√n

The horizontal axis is logarithmic. By the law of large numbers, X̄n → μ. The shaded band μ ± 2σ/√n comes from the central limit theorem: for each fixed n, X̄n lands inside it with probability about 0.95 once n is large. A single run still leaves the band from time to time (by the law of the iterated logarithm it does so infinitely often), so one path is not expected to stay inside. The Central limit theorem tab shows the distribution of X̄n itself.

Distribution of the standardized sum Zn

The horizontal axis is fixed at −4 ≤ z ≤ 4 for every n, so you watch the shape change rather than the scale. Integer-valued sums are drawn as bars of width 1/(σ√n) and height P(Sn = k) · σ√n, so their total area is 1 and they are comparable with the normal density.

How fast the gap closes

Largest CDF gap sup |Fn − Φ| (exact) Berry–Esseen bound 0.4748 · E|X − μ|³ / (σ³√n)

Both axes are logarithmic, so a 1/√n rate is a straight line of slope −1/2. Skewed distributions converge at that rate. Symmetric continuous ones such as the uniform converge faster, about 1/n. Integer-valued sums stay at order 1/√n even when symmetric, because their CDF jumps by roughly 0.4/(σ√n) at every value.

Poisson process with rate λ

Arrivals happen one at a time, independently, at an average rate of λ per unit time; N(t) counts the arrivals in [0, t]. Three equivalent descriptions: the gaps between arrivals are independent Exponential(λ); the number of arrivals in any window of length w is Poisson(λw); and counts in disjoint windows are independent. The page simulates 400 independent paths and compares them with these facts.

Things to try
  • Slide s with w fixed. The window histogram stays Poisson(λw), because the increments are stationary.
  • Slide s and watch the wait histogram. It stays Exponential(λ) wherever you start, which is memorylessness again.
  • Compare the mean gap, 1/λ, with the mean length of the gap that contains time s, which is close to 2/λ. Long gaps are more likely to cover a given moment. This is the inspection paradox.
  • Make λw large. Poisson(λw) starts to look normal, since the count is a sum of many small independent counts (see the Central limit theorem tab).
